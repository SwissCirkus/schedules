# Production knowledge for building schedules

The schedule assistant reads this every time it answers. It is how the studio actually runs jobs (Christian's own process, recorded
2026-11-13), plus common industry practice for live action, short films and features. Durations are rough starting points:
everything is dragged into shape afterwards. Lengths are working days unless said otherwise.

## Principles

- Work in phases with **gates**: a later phase starts only after the gate before it is signed off. Gates are Sign-off WIPs on the
  schedule. The big ones: script lock, cast lock, location lock, PPM (pre-production meeting), look sign-off, animatic sign-off,
  plates lock, picture lock (offline approval), final approval.
- Link rows that follow each other, so moving one pushes the rest. Overlap stages where people really overlap (animation, lighting
  and compositing run together towards the end).
- Client WIPs are usually weekly; internal WIPs more often. Put a client WIP at each gate.
- Leave the last days for grade, sound mix and online / deliverables. Public holidays and skipped weekends are not working days.
- Voiceover: a guide VO early (with the animatic). For character animation the voices are recorded **before** animating, for lip
  sync. Music: a guide track early, final music and sound design towards the end, Foley & SFX with it, the mix just before online.
- Script development can come before anything else on any job.
- Commercials stay in one timeline (they run 4–10 weeks). Short films, features and series are long: group their rows into phases
  (Development, Pre-production, Production, Post-production, Delivery) as blocks with their own schedules inside.
- Crew splits into **above the line** (writers, director, producers, principal cast, voice talent) and **below the line** (every other
  crew role, plus kit, locations and post).

## Commercials (TVC, online films) — 4 to 10 weeks

1. Bid and award: brief, director's treatment, (triple) bid, award. Then a pre-bid / kick-off with agency and client.
2. Pre-production (1–3 weeks before the shoot): casting brief → casting session → callbacks → client approval = **cast lock**;
   location recce → **location lock**; wardrobe, props and art department; shooting board; schedule; permits; insurance.
   **Tech recce** and camera tests 1–2 days before the shoot. **PPM** 2–5 working days before the shoot: production, agency and
   client approve cast, locations, wardrobe, props, shooting board and schedule. Call sheets go out after the PPM.
3. Shoot: usually 1–3 days.
4. Post: footage ingest (a day), offline edit (about a week, ~3 rounds of revisions, longer for a longer shoot) → offline approval
   (picture lock); VFX / online; grade; VO record; music and sound design; final mix; client approval of the online; deliverables
   (versions and cutdowns, captions, slates / clocks, QC, broadcast specs); clearances and talent usage.

## Short film — about 3 to 9 months

- Development: script and rewrites, table read, budget, funding, attaching director / cast, crew hire.
- Pre-production (4–8 weeks): casting and cast lock, locations and location lock, script breakdown and shooting schedule (stripboard),
  permits and insurance, design and wardrobe fittings, rehearsals, table read with cast, tech scout and camera tests in the last days.
- Production: 2–7 shoot days.
- Post (2–4 months): ingest and dailies, assembly, director's cut, picture lock, VFX, conform, grade, sound edit (dialogue, ADR,
  Foley, effects), music spotting and composition, final mix, titles and credits.
- Delivery: master, DCP, subtitles, M&E stems; festival submissions.

## Feature film — about 1 to 2 years (independent)

- Development (months to years): script, rights, financing, packaging (director and cast), budget, greenlight.
- Pre-production (8–16 weeks): heads of department hired, script breakdown, schedule (one-liner / stripboard), locations, casting
  and cast deals, production design and builds, costume fittings, VFX previs, stunts, rehearsals, camera tests, permits, insurance,
  production meetings, tech scout, the full PPM.
- Principal photography (4–12 weeks, ~25–60 shoot days, 5-day weeks), second unit, pick-ups.
- Post (6–12 months): dailies, editor's assembly, director's cut, producer / studio cut, test screenings, picture lock; VFX turnover
  and deliveries; conform; grade (2–4 weeks); sound: spotting, dialogue edit, ADR, Foley, effects, music composition and recording,
  temp mix, final mix (2–4 weeks).
- Delivery and release: deliverables (DCP, masters, stems, M&E, captions, QC), trailer, key art, EPK, festivals, distribution.

## 3D animation

Script / storyboard → 3D animatic (signed off) → concept and character design → modeling (character, props, environment) → rigging
and texturing (hair & fur and cloth for complex characters) → animation → lighting & rendering (FX where needed) → compositing →
grade → online. Character count matters: reusable characters count once; many characters means more modeling and rigging.

## 3D with live action

Before the shoot: concept, character design, storyboard, the 3D animatic signed off before the shoot; character / props modeling,
rigging and texturing (hair & fur, cloth for complex characters). Many characters can run past the shoot. Plus the live-action prep
above (casting, cast lock, recce, location lock, PPM). The shoot (1–3 days for an ad). After it: a day of ingest, the offline edit
(about a week, longer for longer shoots), tracking & roto (keying if actors are on green screen); animation starts about two days
into tracking (shots need tracking first); lighting & rendering, FX and compositing overlap animation towards the end; heavy
compositing at the end; grade, online.

## AI

Look development → character look (character sheets) → environment look → key frames locked with the client → (3D animation guides /
playblasts, sometimes shown to the client as a Playblast review) → draft generation → full generation → upscale & clean-up →
compositing, grade, online.

- **AI with 3D**: treat the AI plates like a shoot. Ask which part is AI (usually backgrounds AI, characters 3D). Character sheets;
  background frames, signed off; plates generated from them with camera moves (3D camera guides / playblasts steer them), upscaled,
  signed off (an important gate); then tracking, animation, integration, roto, compositing, finish. The 3D characters are built
  alongside the AI work.
- **AI with live action**: storyboard (maybe a rough 3D animatic), concept / background frames first; shoot early; ingest, edit,
  keying and roto of the actors (or none, for set extensions); AI plates (backgrounds, set extensions); compositing.
- **Motion graphics with AI** (one or two people): script assumed locked; a little concept and styleframes; a block-in of the whole
  piece with rough placeholders; then AI generation, animation and compositing built up together until the end.

## 2D

- **Character / cel**: storyboard → 2D animatic; character and environment design, signed off; layout (shots locked); key poses,
  signed off; in-betweens → clean-up & colour. Background breakdown & camera moves (backgrounds cut into elements, moves added) is a
  separate job alongside; animation is done with or without the move. Compositing and grade at the end. Lock everything before
  animating.
- **Motion graphics**: concept, styleframes, block-in, then animation and compositing built up together.
- **Cut-out / rigged**: character design, 2D rigging, 2D animation, compositing.

## Stop motion and miniatures

- **Stop motion**: a strict storyboard; sets, puppets and props built (3D printing common); look stills with placeholder characters,
  signed off; set lighting, signed off; the shoot shot by shot with a sign-off per shot; wire & rig removal; grade; Foley, music.
- **Stop-motion characters on 3D backgrounds**: 3D sets modeled and lit first; puppets shot on green screen matching the 3D camera;
  keyed and composited.
- **Stop-motion characters on live-action plates**: plates filmed first; puppets on green screen matching the moves (usually no
  tracking); keyed and composited.
- **Miniatures only**: storyboard, 2D / 3D animatic; rough cardboard / foam-board blockout; the miniature build (the biggest part,
  3D printed parts alongside); lighting; the model shoot at the end (sometimes with non-animated puppets); clean-up, stitching shots,
  compositing, grade, music, sound effects.
- **Miniatures with 3D characters**: storyboard, 3D animatic, concept signed off; the model built, lit and signed off; background
  plates filmed (moves or stills), photogrammetry of the model for shadow catching; 3D characters modeled, textured and rigged
  meanwhile; when plates and characters are ready, track the plates, animate, light, composite, grade, sound.

## Live-action checklist (any job with a shoot)

Casting brief, casting session, callbacks, client approval → cast lock; location recce → location lock; wardrobe and props; art
department / builds; storyboard or shooting board; permits, insurance; tech recce and camera tests 1–2 days before; PPM a few days
before; call sheets; shoot; DIT / ingest; offline; online; grade; sound; deliverables.
